CRYPTOGRAPHIC SPECIFICATION

How VaultPass protects your vault

Encryption and decryption happen on your own devices. There is no VaultPass server, and your master password is never transmitted.

1 Key hierarchy

Step 1: Master Key (KDF)

Your master password runs through PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 for 300,000 iterations with a random 16-byte salt, producing a 256-bit Key Encryption Key (KEK).

Makes each password guess expensive
Step 2: Dual DEK Wrapping

A random Data Encryption Key (DEK) encrypts your entries. The DEK is stored twice: wrapped with the KEK, and, if you turn on biometric unlock, wrapped with an Android Keystore key.

Keystore is hardware-backed (TEE or StrongBox) on phones that have it
Step 3: AES-256-GCM Payloads

Credential records, notes and custom fields in the local Room (SQLite) database are encrypted with AES-GCM and carry a 128-bit authentication tag.

A modified record fails the tag check instead of decrypting

Constant-Time Verification

An ordinary comparison stops at the first wrong byte, so its timing can leak how close a guess was. VaultPass checks the password hash with MessageDigest.isEqual(), which takes the same time whether the bytes match or not.

Key Wiped on Lock

When you lock the vault, or the auto-lock timer fires (tracked with ProcessLifecycleOwner), VaultPass overwrites its copy of the data key with zeros. That wipes the key itself; decrypted entries held as Java strings can't be overwritten this way and are freed by Android later.

Incremental Lockout Protection

Failed attempts are counted in DataStore on disk, so restarting the app doesn't reset them:

  • • 5 failed attempts: 30-second cooldown
  • • 10 failed attempts: 60-second cooldown
  • • 15 failed attempts: 5-minute lockout
  • • 20+ failed attempts: 15-minute lockout

Screenshot Blocking & Clipboard Clearing

VaultPass sets FLAG_SECURE on its own screens, which blocks screenshots and blanks the app in the Recent Apps switcher. From version 2.7.0 this is on by default. To allow screenshots, turn off Disable Screenshots in Settings. Copied passwords are cleared from the clipboard after a timer you choose (30 seconds by default).

Sync with the Desktop App (Optional)

VaultPass can sync with the VaultPass desktop app for Windows. You pair the two by scanning a QR code, and they exchange changes directly over your local network. Nothing passes through the internet or a server.

The .vpex encrypted backup

An encrypted export produces a .vpex file. Each file has its own random 16-byte salt and IV; the key is derived from a password with PBKDF2, and the contents are encrypted with AES-GCM and Base64-encoded. The GCM authentication tag makes a modified file fail to decrypt. You can keep a .vpex on a USB drive or your computer without exposing plaintext. JSON and TXT exports are not encrypted.
