Cloud Password Managers

  • ✕ Centralized target: Your encrypted vault sits on the provider's servers next to everyone else's.
  • ✕ Account required: You sign up with an email, and the provider knows who you are.
  • ✕ Subscriptions: Many charge monthly for features such as sync across devices.
  • ✕ Depends on their servers: Sync and sign-in need the provider to be up and reachable.

VaultPass Architecture

  • ✓ No cloud: Your vault lives on your phone. The only sync is the optional one with the VaultPass desktop app, over your local network.
  • ✓ No tracking: No analytics SDKs, no ad trackers, no account registration.
  • ✓ Keystore-backed biometrics: The biometric unlock key is kept in the Android Keystore, which is hardware-backed on phones that support it.
  • ✓ Checkable download: The APK's SHA-256 is listed on this site, with a VirusTotal lookup for that exact file.

What protects your vault

PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 & AES-256-GCM

Your master password goes through 300,000 rounds of PBKDF2 before it can unlock the key that encrypts your entries with AES-256-GCM.

// Cryptographic Parameters
Algorithm: PBKDF2WithHmacSHA256
Work Factor: 300,000 rounds
Cipher: AES/GCM/NoPadding (256-bit)

Biometric Unlock

Unlock with your fingerprint or face. The unlock key stays in the Android Keystore.

Native Android Autofill

Fills logins in apps and browsers through Android's Autofill framework, so passwords don't pass through the clipboard.

Security Center

Flags weak and reused passwords, and entries with no password, with a health score for the whole vault.

Anti-Snoop Shield

Screenshot blocking (on by default from 2.7.0), a clipboard that clears itself on a timer, and auto-lock that drops the vault key from memory.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you recover my master password if I forget it?

No. There is no server and no recovery key. Your vault is encrypted with AES-GCM using a key derived from your master password (PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256), so nobody, including the developer, can reset a forgotten master password.

How does the Android Autofill feature work?

VaultPass registers as an Android Autofill service. When an app or website shows a login form, it walks the screen's view structure to find the username and password fields, then suggests matching entries. In a known browser, an entry matches when its website is on the same domain as the page. In other apps, VaultPass offers only entries you linked to that app: pick one once with "Search VaultPass…" and it is offered there from then on.

How do I back up my passwords without a cloud?

Export an encrypted .vpex backup. The file is AES-GCM encrypted with a key derived from a password (PBKDF2), so you can keep it on a USB drive or your computer. JSON and TXT exports also exist, but those are unencrypted.

What if someone keeps guessing my master password?

VaultPass counts failed attempts on disk, so restarting the app doesn't reset the count. After 5 failures it locks input for 30 seconds, after 10 for 60 seconds, after 15 for 5 minutes, and after 20 or more for 15 minutes.

Does VaultPass use the internet?

Your vault is never sent to the internet or to a cloud service. There are two optional network features. Sync with the VaultPass desktop app for Windows: you pair the two by scanning a QR code, and they exchange changes directly over your local network. And an update check, off by default, that asks GitHub for the latest version and sends nothing from your vault.
